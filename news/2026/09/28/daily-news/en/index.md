## 1. Energy | Oil rises, but contract months matter

**Facts:** During Asian trading on September 28, the US and Iran remained divided over conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Bloomberg’s 04:54 UTC snapshot put November Brent at $107.02/bbl, up 2.6%, and December at $99.18, up 1.8%. These are different maturities; the November contract expires Wednesday. Reuters and AP also reported higher oil prices.

**Market impact · inference:** Inference: Tight near-term supply can raise transport and input costs and complicate monetary policy. The front-month premium also cautions against treating immediate scarcity as a full-year oil-price forecast.

**Counter-risks:** Counter-risk: Further talks, restored physical flows or weaker demand could reduce the premium. Diplomatic statements do not establish verified shipping volumes; this briefing does not claim a full reopening.

## 3. Macro | China’s industrial profits grow more slowly

**Facts:** China’s statistics bureau reported on September 28 that August industrial profits rose 4.2% year over year, versus 11.2% in July; January–August profits grew 15.7%. It cited a high comparison base. Revenue rose 6.6% over the first eight months; finished-goods inventory turnover took 0.6 day longer and receivables collection 0.9 day longer than a year earlier. Coverage is restricted to industrial enterprises above the designated size.

**Market impact · inference:** Inference: Aggregate profit growth can coexist with inventory and collection pressure. Assessments of industrial and commodity demand should distinguish high-tech manufacturing strength from broader demand.

**Counter-risks:** Counter-risk: Slower annual growth does not establish a sequential contraction; base effects and lower costs may obscure resilience. Conversely, nominal profit growth does not guarantee stronger cash flow.

## 4. Central banks | Australia’s decision precedes its CPI release

**Facts:** As checked on September 28, the RBA’s official cash-rate target remained 4.35%, with the next update due September 29 at 14:30 AEST (04:30 UTC). The latest released July CPI showed 3.5% annual inflation and 3.6% trimmed-mean inflation. The statistics bureau schedules August CPI for September 30. Today’s reporting widely anticipates a 25-basis-point hike; no decision has been announced.

**Market impact · inference:** Inference: Guidance on the subsequent path may matter as much as the decision. A policy meeting followed by inflation data creates two consecutive opportunities to reprice rates and the Australian dollar.

**Counter-risks:** Counter-risk: An expected hike may already be discounted. Cautious guidance, weak demand or softer inflation could weigh on the currency. A move to 4.60% is a forecast, not an accomplished fact.

## 6. Companies | Rejected gold-mining approach highlights moving consideration

**Facts:** Public reports on September 28 said Northern Star rejected Gold Fields’ approach. The bidder’s same-day exchange announcement confirms a conditional, non-binding proposal: 0.3125 Gold Fields shares plus A$7.25 cash per target share. Initially valued at A$27 per share, or about A$38.7 billion in equity, its implied value was A$25.19 using the bidder’s Friday share price, according to Reuters. No transaction has been agreed.

**Market impact · inference:** Inference: The case shifts attention from bullion to asset portfolios, integration costs and the value of share consideration. A proposed premium can support expectations but is not a guaranteed cash floor.

**Counter-risks:** Counter-risk: The target cited undervaluation and jurisdictional exposure. Claimed synergies remain the bidder’s estimates. Disengagement, approvals, financing or share-price changes could undermine deal expectations.
