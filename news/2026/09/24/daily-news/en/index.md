## 1. Australian employment rises, but unemployment reaches 4.6%

Macro | Released September 24, 11:30 AEST; August data

**Fact:** Seasonally adjusted employment rose 39,500, while unemployment reached 4.6% and participation 67.1%. Full-time employment fell 6,300. More people were both employed and unemployed as labour supply expanded.

**Market impact | Inference:** Additional labour supply could ease wage pressure and complicate next week’s rate decision. Rising unemployment alone does not establish falling employment.

**Countercase | Scenario:** Hours worked increased 0.7%, so demand may remain resilient. The statistics agency flags possible monthly seasonal effects from survey changes; trend estimates matter.

## 2. Switzerland keeps a zero rate despite energy inflation

Central banks / FX | September 24 policy assessment

**Fact:** The Swiss central bank held its policy rate at 0% and retained willingness to intervene in FX markets. August inflation was 0.8%. Its conditional inflation forecasts are 0.7% for 2026 and 0.8% for 2027.

**Market impact | Inference:** Lower inflation permits policy divergence. Interest-rate differentials could constrain the franc, without guaranteeing its direction.

**Countercase | Scenario:** Dearer energy or further currency weakness could lift imported inflation; safe-haven demand could instead strengthen the franc. Forecasts assume a constant zero rate, not a commitment.

## 3. Dollar stays near a two-month high as short rates reprice

Rates / FX | September 24, 08:50 BST report snapshot

**Fact:** The morning wire report put the dollar index at 101.05, slightly lower on the day. Wednesday’s 101.23 was its highest since July 29. The same report said two-year Treasury yields reached their highest since May 2024.

**Market impact | Inference:** Stronger US activity and energy costs may reinforce expectations of sustained tightening, supporting the dollar and raising funding hurdles. This is transmission analysis.

**Countercase | Scenario:** Softer data or a diplomatic breakthrough could reverse the move. Yen intervention risk also limits one-way positioning. These are reported snapshots, not synchronized cutoff prices.

## 4. Oil rises again; diesel export restrictions remain unsettled

Energy / industry | September 24, 08:03 UTC prices

**Fact:** Brent futures were $105.40 a barrel, up 2.25%; US crude futures were $93.94, up 1.93%. Reporting indicated little diplomatic progress with Iran. The White House denied a report about preparations for a 90-day diesel export ban; no effective ban is asserted here.

**Market impact | Inference:** Higher oil could raise transport and manufacturing costs. Export restrictions, if implemented, could widen domestic-versus-overseas diesel spreads and affect refinery output.

**Countercase | Scenario:** Diplomatic progress and restored supply could lower crude prices. Policy text, exemptions and execution remain unsettled. Crude and refined products need not move together.

## 5. Japanese technology lifts the index while Europe weakens

Equities / sectors | Japanese September 24 close; Europe at 07:10 UTC

**Fact:** Japan’s main 225-stock index closed up 0.76% at 65,513.99, while the broader index fell 0.39%. Component stocks benefited from AI hardware demand expectations. Europe’s main 600-stock index fell 0.4% at 07:10 UTC; energy shares gained 0.3%.

**Market impact | Inference:** Diverging index performance suggests selective growth exposure. Energy costs weigh differently on importing economies; a technology rally is not evidence of broad risk appetite.

**Countercase | Scenario:** Hardware expectations require orders and profits. Cheaper oil could change sector leadership. The Japanese close and European intraday snapshot are not simultaneous; US cash trading has yet to open.

## 6. Swedish apparel retailer beats on profit, but shares fall

Companies / consumers | September 24, 08:00 CEST release; early European trading

**Fact:** A Swedish apparel retailer reported June–August operating profit of SEK 6.037 billion versus a wire-service consensus of SEK 5.14 billion. Gross margin was 54.0%, including roughly 1.6 percentage points of one-time benefits. Local-currency sales rose 1%, with the same growth expected for September. Shares fell 2.5% in early European trading.

**Market impact | Inference:** Investors may prioritize sales momentum and recurring earnings. The 1.6-point benefit is a margin contribution, not a profit growth rate.

**Countercase | Scenario:** Cost discipline and better sales could sustain recovery; supply disruption and fading one-time benefits could hurt it. September sales remain a company expectation, not a completed result.
