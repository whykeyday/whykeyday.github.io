01

China manufacturing contracts again as orders lag output

FactsAt 09:30 Beijing time on October 31, the official manufacturing PMI was released at 49.3, versus 50.1 in September. Output was 49.8 and new orders 49.4. Large firms remained above 50 at 50.2, while medium and small firms were below it. This diffusion index does not imply a 0.7% fall in output.

Market implications | Inference and opinionInference: Orders below output suggest weak demand support for restocking. Industrial commodities, exporters and regional risk assets may become more dependent on policy support. This is a Saturday release; a subsequent equity-market reaction has not yet been observed.

Counter-risksCounter-risk: Holiday effects and seasonal adjustment can affect one monthly survey. Expansion among large firms, policy support or returning orders could change the trend. Industrial and trade data are needed before drawing recession conclusions.

02

Euro area growth slows without stalling

FactsThe October 30 preliminary estimate put seasonally adjusted euro area real GDP growth at 0.4% quarter on quarter in Q3, down from 0.6% in Q2, and 1.3% year on year. The Reuters poll expected 0.2% quarterly growth. These quarterly figures are not annualized and are not directly comparable with annualized US growth.

Market implications | Inference and opinionInference: Better-than-expected growth reduces the immediate case for stimulus and may support revenue expectations in cyclical industries. Slower growth alongside energy costs still leaves monetary easing dependent on inflation. The GDP result alone does not establish a bullish currency outcome.

Counter-risksCounter-risk: The estimate uses incomplete data and is subject to revision. Uneven regional performance, real purchasing power and energy prices could weaken subsequent demand. Aggregate growth does not establish equal strength across member economies.

Stronger Treasuries and a softer dollar do not establish a policy pivot

FactsReuters reported on October 30 that declining Treasury yields weighed on the dollar and the yen strengthened after the Bank of Japan held rates. The policy backdrop remains the October 28 Fed decision: a 10–2 vote to hold the target range at 3.75%–4.00%, with two votes for a 25-basis-point increase. That decision is context from this week, not a new decision today.

Market implications | Inference and opinionInference: Safety demand for bonds can diverge temporarily from policy-rate expectations. A bond rally should not automatically be interpreted as a rate-cut trade. Dollar weakness may ease some dollar funding pressure, but exchange rates also depend on relative interest rates and cross-border flows.

Counter-risksCounter-risk: Persistent energy inflation or stronger employment could lift rate expectations again. The dollar can also regain support during risk aversion. Currency and yield levels without a verified common timestamp are excluded from the closing snapshot.

Energy profits shift toward refining: XOM results expose supply constraints

FactsXOM disclosed Q3 net income of $15.4 billion, or $3.72 per share, on October 30. Adjusted earnings were $17.5 billion, or $4.23 per share. Operating cash flow was $25.7 billion and free cash flow $19.1 billion. Reuters reported stronger refining margins but a 20% quarterly decline in adjusted upstream earnings; the two segments did not improve together.

Market implications | Inference and opinionInference: Limited fuel-processing capacity can move profits from crude production toward refining. Integrated producers benefiting does not establish that the energy cost shock has ended. Transport, chemicals and consumers may remain exposed. Operating cash flow, capital spending and shareholder distributions should be assessed separately; adjusted earnings are not GAAP net income.

Counter-risksCounter-risk: Refinery restarts, stock releases or weaker demand could compress processing margins. Strong quarterly cash flow cannot be extrapolated mechanically to a full year or used to establish continuing growth in buybacks or dividends. The pace of supply restoration matters for profit durability.
