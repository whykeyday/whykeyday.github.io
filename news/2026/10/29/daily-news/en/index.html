01 · The Fed holds; two voters prefer a hike

Decision: October 28, 14:00 EDT; reviewed for this morning.

Facts: The Fed held its target range at 3.75%–4.00% by a 10–2 vote. Two voters preferred a quarter-point increase. The statement described strong growth and elevated inflation. US indexes finished higher Wednesday.

Market impact | inference and opinion: Inference: the hold reduces the immediate policy shock, while dissent leaves tightening possible. Valuations still depend on earnings and yields. Today’s GDP and PCE releases were pending at cutoff.

Counter-risk: Persistent inflation could shorten the pause. Weaker demand or prices could reverse hike expectations. Dissent is not a commitment to future action.

02 · Canada raises its rate to 2.50%, diverging from the US

Decision: October 28; latest formal outcome at this morning’s cutoff.

Facts: The Bank of Canada raised its overnight target by 25 basis points to 2.50%; the Bank Rate is 2.75% and deposit rate 2.45%. Reuters reported that further increases remained possible to contain broader inflation from energy costs.

Market impact | inference and opinion: Inference: the marginal rate differential may support CAD while increasing floating-rate borrowing and property funding costs. This is a mechanism, not a claim that CAD already rose because of the decision.

Counter-risk: Oil, safe-haven USD demand and domestic growth also matter. Cheaper energy or weaker consumption could limit further tightening. One hike does not establish a certain cycle.

04 · Oil gives back gains; physical recovery still needs evidence

Reuters update: October 29, 00:29 GMT; background outlook: October.

Facts: Reuters quoted WTI futures at $101.86 a barrel, down 0.9%, after Wednesday’s roughly 3% rise. AP also reported Thursday declines. EIA’s October outlook assumes constrained Middle East flows; inputs ended October 1 and do not confirm today’s output.

Market impact | inference and opinion: Inference: diplomatic hopes can reduce the risk premium, but shipping, exports and refinery recovery determine lasting fuel relief. Lower oil may ease inflation and transport margin pressure.

Counter-risk: Reports of attacks do not quantify verified lost production. Contract months and benchmarks differ. Failed talks could lift costs again; weak demand can also lower oil without supply recovery.

05 · Cloud earnings split: separate growth, accounting and cash

Results after the October 28 US close; extended trading is not today’s close.

Facts: MSFT reported fiscal Q1 revenue of $92.3 billion, up 19%, GAAP EPS of $4.92 and adjusted EPS of $4.89 excluding investment effects. Reuters reported MSFT higher and GOOGL lower after hours. AP reported revenue growth but an expectations miss and investment losses at GOOGL.

Market impact | inference and opinion: Inference: AI sales alone do not explain returns. Cash generation, capital spending and expectations matter. Editorial view: compare operating performance alongside investment valuation changes.

Counter-risk: Adjusted EPS is neither superior to GAAP nor free cash flow. Investment marks can reverse. Thin extended trading does not validate a long-term business model.

06 · Record memory profits coexist with device and expansion risks

Formal Q3 results released in Korea on October 29; distinct from early-October guidance.

Facts: Ticker 005930 reported revenue of KRW 195.3 trillion and operating profit of KRW 107.4 trillion. Its memory business set records, but the device division lost KRW 0.8 trillion. Quarterly capital expenditure was KRW 29 trillion. AP and Reuters separately reported the profit record.

Market impact | inference and opinion: Inference: strong memory economics do not imply equal benefits for electronics customers. Higher chip costs can squeeze downstream margins; utilization and deliveries will test expansion returns.

Counter-risk: Slower AI spending, new capacity or lower prices could hit cyclical profits. Management demand guidance is a forecast. Record operating profit alone does not explain equity returns.
