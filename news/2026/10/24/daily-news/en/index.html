Daily Finance | Growth meets the cost test

Weekend briefing · October 24, 2026 · Information cutoff: 11:00 UTC / 07:00 EDT in Toronto. Today is Saturday; market prices refer to the October 23 session, not live Saturday trading.

Editorial view: US activity remains resilient while European growth moderates. Energy and credit costs make strong growth an ambiguous signal for markets. Next week’s central-bank meetings will test that divergence.

01 · US growth stays strong as selling prices accelerate

Macro · Released October 23

Facts: The October flash composite PMI was 57.6, down from 58.4 in September but above the 50 expansion threshold. Survey selling prices rose at their fastest pace since August 2022.

Market impact · Inference: Resilient activity and cost pass-through may limit room for lower rates. Revenue growth may not fully offset the valuation effect of a higher discount rate.

Countercase · Scenario: PMI is a business survey, not realized GDP or CPI. Flash readings can change and precautionary demand may fade. It does not establish that a rate increase is inevitable next week.

02 · Eurozone growth slows, complicating the euro and rate outlook

Macro / FX · Released October 23

Facts: The eurozone flash composite PMI fell from 53.1 to 51.9, with French activity contracting again. FXStreet reported a weaker euro against the dollar at 08:06 UTC after the release; that was an intraday observation, not a closing quote.

Market impact · Inference: Slower growth could lower rate expectations and weigh on the euro, while energy costs complicate the policy response. Divergent US and European growth may amplify currency volatility.

Countercase · Scenario: A reading of 51.9 still signals expansion, not a eurozone recession. Persistent inflation could support the euro through interest-rate differentials, and French disruptions may prove temporary.

03 · Technology steadies locally, but stocks fall and yields rise

Equities / Rates · October 23 New York close and closing reports

Facts: The S&P 500 closed at 7,623.68, the Dow at 52,215.16 and the Nasdaq at 25,955.34, all lower. Reuters reported a 0.4% gain in the semiconductor index and a rebound in the US 10-year Treasury yield to about 4.99%.

Market impact · Inference: Limited technology stabilization has yet to lift the broader market. Higher financing costs still affect earnings and valuations; next week requires scrutiny of both results and bond pricing.

Countercase · Scenario: One session of sector divergence cannot establish a reversal. Easing energy risk and lower yields could relieve pressure, while renewed cost stress could undermine the rebound.

04 · Oil rises while parts of the physical market soften

Energy · October 23 settlements and reporting

Facts: Brent futures settled at $116.72 a barrel and WTI at $103.27. Separately, Bloomberg reported lower physical prices for some Middle Eastern crude sold outside the Persian Gulf as shipping adapted.

Market impact · Inference: A futures risk premium can coexist with improving cargo flows. Higher energy costs may squeeze transport, manufacturing and household spending, but futures gains do not measure actual lost supply.

Countercase · Scenario: Stable deliveries could unwind the premium; wider attacks could overwhelm shipping adaptations. The October 6 energy outlook is an earlier forecast, not a Friday supply measurement. Sources disagree on the historical-high comparison, which is omitted here.

Sources (identified by origin):

  • Reuters settlements and supply-risk reporting · Oct 23

05 · Regional banking: better interest income meets higher credit costs

Company / Banking · Third-quarter results released October 23

Facts: Flagstar reported $3 million of income attributable to common shareholders and diluted EPS of $0.01, versus $0.06 in the second quarter. Credit-loss provisions rose from $18 million to $76 million, while net interest income increased 5% sequentially.

Market impact · Inference: Higher rates do not automatically improve a bank’s bottom line. Interest income, funding costs and credit losses need to be assessed together.

Countercase · Scenario: Net charge-offs fell 26% sequentially, so higher provisions do not mean realized write-offs deteriorated in parallel. One bank cannot represent the sector. The two news reports use different revenue estimates; no revenue beat-or-miss conclusion is adopted.
