# Daily Finance | 2026-10-01

## Softer inflation, divided markets

Cutoff: 07:00 EDT / 11:00 UTC

Editorial view: Softer-than-expected inflation, manufacturing resilience and chip earnings offer support. Long-bond pressure and uneven service-sector signals still argue against a simple broad-easing narrative.

## 1. Macro | Inflation undershoots expectations; spending stays firm

**Facts:** Released September 30 at 08:30 EDT, the August figures show headline PCE inflation of 3.4% year over year and core inflation of 3.0%. Monthly increases were 0.3% and 0.2%, respectively; real spending rose 0.6%. These are premarket inputs for today, not October inflation.

**Market impact | Inference:** Inference: Softer-than-expected inflation eases pressure for another near-term rate increase. Resilient spending, however, offers little evidence of a demand collapse. Equity valuation relief depends partly on longer-term yields.

**Counter-risk:** Counter-risk: Monthly inflation accelerated from revised July readings. An upside surprise relative to disinflation hopes remains possible. This release includes annual revisions; an inconsistent annual figure in an early syndicated report was excluded in favor of the official release, cross-checked against two wire reports.

## 2. Rates and FX | Japan shows a split business cycle

**Facts:** The October 1 Tankan, released at 08:50 JST, put large manufacturers’ sentiment at 24, up from 22, while large non-manufacturers fell to 35 from 37. These are favorable-minus-unfavorable diffusion indexes, not output growth rates.

**Market impact | Inference:** Inference: Manufacturing resilience supports policy normalization, while weaker service-sector sentiment makes consecutive rate increases less certain. The yen remains sensitive to both domestic policy and the U.S.–Japan rate differential.

**Counter-risk:** Counter-risk: Currency weakness and imported energy costs could keep inflation pressure elevated. Persistent weakness outside manufacturing would instead challenge domestic demand. Survey sentiment is not realized profit.

## 3. Industry | European factories expand, with cost constraints

**Facts:** The euro-area September manufacturing PMI, published October 1, was finalized at 52.9, above both August and the preliminary reading of 52.7. It remains above the 50 expansion threshold. The Dutch manufacturing PMI reached 55.6, adding regional context.

**Market impact | Inference:** Inference: Better equipment demand supports industrial suppliers, but strength in capital goods does not establish a broad consumer recovery. Order conversion into production, delivery and cash receipts matters more than the headline alone.

**Counter-risk:** Counter-risk: Energy and input costs can erode margins; Dutch strength cannot be generalized across member states. Two separate editorial sources corroborate the euro-area final reading. The compiler’s original daily release was not directly retrieved; bank research supplies Dutch and sector context, not a third independent measurement.

At 03:34 EDT, Dow futures were down about 0.4% while Nasdaq 100 futures gained about 0.5%. A separate 04:06 EDT market report also described mixed futures.

**Market impact | Inference:** Inference: Chip earnings offer selective support rather than a broad rally. Lower odds of an immediate policy hike can coexist with pressure on long bonds: term, inflation and supply risks differ from the next central-bank decision.

**Counter-risk:** Counter-risk: Premarket and holiday-period moves can reverse quickly. These observations have different timestamps and are neither synchronized live quotes nor today’s closing prices. Higher yields or energy costs could also challenge technology shares.

## 5. Company | MU delivers growth; guidance remains a forecast

**Facts:** The U.S. memory-chip producer (MU) reported after the September 30 close. Revenue for the quarter ended September 3 was $54.23 billion; GAAP diluted EPS was $32.87 and adjusted EPS was $33.42. Next-quarter revenue guidance is $60–63 billion, a management forecast rather than completed sales.

**Market impact | Inference:** Inference: The results offer company-level evidence of AI memory demand and help explain relative strength in technology futures. They do not establish equivalent margins for other suppliers.

**Counter-risk:** Counter-risk: Adjusted gross-margin guidance of approximately 86.25% is below the quarter’s 87.0%. A high comparison base, customer spending and capacity expansion could change earnings momentum. Analyst consensus figures differ between reports; this brief does not combine them into a single earnings-surprise calculation.
