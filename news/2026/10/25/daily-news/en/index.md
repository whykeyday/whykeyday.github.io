## 01 · France keeps its rating; fiscal constraints remain

Macro / sovereign credit · Event: Oct 23; follow-up: Oct 24

**Facts：** The finance ministry, Reuters and Le Monde confirm that France retains its Aa3 rating and negative outlook. The ministry targets a 2027 deficit of 5% of GDP; this is a budget objective, not an achieved outcome.

**Market impact | inference：** Avoiding a downgrade could ease immediate pressure on French sovereign risk premiums and financial-sector valuations. It does not establish a weekend rally in bonds or the euro.

**Countercase and risks | scenarios：** The negative outlook remains. Budget passage and delivery matter; global rates and debt issuance could still raise funding costs even without a downgrade.

**Source independence：** Three separate organizations: a primary government statement and two newsrooms. The government’s assessment of its budget credibility is its own position.

## 02 · Central-bank week: a pause would not establish an easing turn

Rates / FX · Calendars checked Oct 25; poll published Oct 20

**Facts：** The Fed meets October 27–28, without a scheduled Summary of Economic Projections. In a Reuters survey conducted October 16–20, 94 of 100 economists expected a hold at 3.75%–4.00%; that is a forecast, not a decision. BEA currently schedules both advance Q3 GDP and September Personal Income and Outlays for October 29 at 08:30 Eastern.

**Market impact | inference：** A more restrictive message than expected could support the dollar and short-term yields while weighing on rate-sensitive equities. Simultaneous GDP and PCE releases could prompt another repricing after the decision.

**Countercase and risks | scenarios：** An economist count is neither a market probability nor a central-bank commitment. Softer growth or inflation could reverse dollar and yield reactions. Calendars can change.

**Source independence：** Four separate organizations. Fed and BEA calendars establish different events; the Reuters survey and Kiplinger preview provide context. The older October 20 poll is explicitly background.

## 05 · Oilfield-services revenue grows while margins need scrutiny

Company / energy services · Q3 results released Oct 23

**Facts：** SLB reported revenue of $9.449 billion, up about 6% year on year. GAAP EPS was $0.49 versus $0.50 a year earlier. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 21.9%, versus 23.1%. Reuters reported an earnings beat; MarketBeat lists adjusted EPS of $0.64 against $0.62 consensus. Adjusted measures do not replace GAAP.

**Market impact | inference：** Revenue growth alongside margin contraction shows why higher oil prices do not mechanically imply stronger year-on-year oilfield-services profitability. Customer spending, project execution and costs matter.

**Countercase and risks | scenarios：** Sequential improvement also matters: adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 21.2% in Q2. Better execution could ease annual margin pressure, whereas delayed customer investment could weaken revenue and cash flow.

**Source independence：** Three separate publishers: the company, Reuters and MarketBeat. Reported actuals originate in the same corporate disclosure, not three independent audits; consensus samples may differ.
