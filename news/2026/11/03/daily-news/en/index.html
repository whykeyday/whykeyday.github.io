Rates, inflation and earnings: the gaps beneath prices

The Australian dollar falls after a rate hike, Korean core inflation holds steady, and technology shares face renewed scrutiny of cash generation. Separate policy actions, changing expectations and recurring earnings.

November 3, 2026 · Cutoff 12:00 UTC / 07:00 EST (Toronto)

01 / Rates / FX

Australia lifts rates to 4.85%, but the currency falls

Facts

On November 3, the Reserve Bank of Australia voted 6–3 to raise its cash rate target by 25 basis points, from 4.60% to 4.85%, effective November 4. It projects inflation reaching the top of its 2%–3% target band only in late 2027. A Reuters FX report published at 09:27 UTC put AUD/USD at 0.6963, down about 0.5%; this is an intraday snapshot.

Market impact | Inference

Inference: currencies respond to changes in the expected rate path. An anticipated hike accompanied by dissent can lower expectations for further tightening. Higher borrowing costs continue to constrain housing and discretionary spending.

Counter-risk | Scenario

Counter-risk: dissent does not establish an end to tightening. Energy costs, services inflation or expectations could lift the rate path again. A sharper demand slowdown could also offset any currency support from higher rates.

02 / Macro / Inflation

Korean inflation eases to 2.8%; underlying pressure persists

Facts

Data released on November 3 show Korean CPI rising 2.8% year on year in October, after 2.9% in September. Core CPI excluding food and energy remained at 2.8%. The finance ministry estimates that petroleum price caps reduced headline inflation by 0.8 percentage points; this is an estimated policy effect, not a separate observed CPI reading.

Market impact | Inference

Inference: modest headline relief helps purchasing power, but unchanged core inflation weakens the case for inferring easing from one release. Bonds and the won remain sensitive to domestic demand, imported energy costs and policy guidance.

Counter-risk | Scenario

Counter-risk: cheaper food and administered price caps can conceal cost pressure, while withdrawal of support would change the inflation path. Sustained cheaper oil or weaker demand could eventually cool core prices. Reported expectations of a rate hike remain forecasts.

03 / Equities / Technology

Korean shares close down 3.59% as AI funding worries spread

Facts

The KOSPI closed November 3 at 5,648.79, down 3.59%, according to a local closing report published at 16:48 KST. On November 2, the S&P 500 fell 0.51% and the Nasdaq Composite lost 1.07%, while the Dow gained 0.41%. Early European shares and US equity futures were also weaker on November 3; futures are not a US cash close.

Market impact | Inference

Inference: concentrated technology exposure makes Asian markets sensitive to expectations for AI returns and funding costs. Index weakness can coexist with sector rotation; market breadth, credit costs and corporate cash generation offer useful cross-checks.

Counter-risk | Scenario

Counter-risk: strong orders or earnings could reverse pessimism. A one-day index decline does not prove weaker end demand. US election outcomes and subsequent policy remain uncertain, so political explanations for intraday moves are provisional.

04 / Sector / Energy

Energy earnings benefit from high prices, with disposal gains in the mix

Facts

Saudi Aramco reported third-quarter net income of about $36.3 billion on November 3, versus $26.9 billion a year earlier. Adjusted net income was $31.2 billion, up 9%, compared with roughly 35% growth in reported profit. Free cash flow was $28.2 billion. Company disclosures and AP reporting identify contributions from an asset disposal and refining margins. Reuters reported a partial Asian-session oil rebound after Monday’s decline of more than 7%.

Market impact | Inference

Inference: crude prices, refining spreads, export routes and exceptional gains need separate assessment. Historical cash generation offers spending and dividend flexibility, but cannot establish a future growth rate. Cheaper oil may benefit fuel-consuming industries such as transport.

Counter-risk | Scenario

Counter-risk: outages, shipping disruption and facility risks can raise costs. Diplomatic progress could reduce both risk premia and refining margins. Monday’s sell-off and Tuesday’s rebound belong to different sessions and cannot be combined into a fresh live quote.

05 / Company / Medical equipment

Medical equipment orders improve, while legal provisions weigh on earnings

Facts

Philips released third-quarter results at 06:00 UTC on November 3. Comparable orders rose 8%, comparable sales increased 3%, and the adjusted EBITA margin gained 70 basis points to 13.0%. Free cash flow was EUR 384 million. A provisional agreement on investigations related to a respiratory-device recall led to a EUR 288 million provision. Documentation remains to be finalized, with payment expected in the first quarter of 2027. Full-year guidance was maintained.

Market impact | Inference

Inference: orders growing faster than sales offer a clue to future revenue, but conversion and cash collection still need verification. Better adjusted margins can coexist with provision-related pressure; statutory earnings and cash flow remain essential checks.

Counter-risk | Scenario

Counter-risk: a provisional agreement does not close all legal exposure, and payment timing may change. Full-year margin guidance includes approximately one percentage point of benefit from US tariff refunds; the entire improvement cannot be treated as recurring operating efficiency.
