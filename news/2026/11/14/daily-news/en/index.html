01 · Producer prices: monthly relief, annual pressure

Facts: The October final-demand PPI release, published November 13 at 08:30 EST, showed a 0.3% monthly rise versus 1.4% in September, and a 6.5% annual rise. The measure excluding food, energy and trade services still rose 0.5% monthly. These are released figures.

Market impact (inference): Inference: monthly moderation may reduce pressure for faster tightening, while persistent prices constrain a policy pivot. Watch cost transmission to consumers; PPI is not a substitute for PCE.

Countercase: Countercase: base effects, energy prices and revisions can change the annual trend. One softer release neither establishes inflation control nor determines the next policy decision.

Source provenance: The official release supplies the data, the news agency checks it, and the research note offers policy interpretation. These are three editorial origins, not three independent inflation measurements.

02 · Rates and currencies: dollar strength survives one release

Facts: The November 13 International Monetary Fund intraday monitor put the US ten-year yield near 5.13%, up 27 basis points over the week, and the dollar index near 103.2. The evening wire report described the dollar at a 19-month high. Intraday snapshots are not closing quotes.

Market impact (inference): Inference: higher risk-free yields increase refinancing costs; a strong dollar strains dollar borrowers and foreign earnings translation. Together they constrain room for equity valuation expansion.

Countercase: Countercase: yields also reflect supply, term premium and growth. Weaker data could reverse both markets. The weekly review and intraday monitor use different cutoffs and are not combined into a precise closing quote.

Source provenance: A multilateral monitor, an international wire and an independent weekly review provide a snapshot, closing direction and weekly context. The weekly review is secondary evidence.

03 · Equities: index weakness masks uneven performance

Facts: At the November 13 US cash close, the S&P 500 fell 0.40%, the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.91%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.10%. Technology weakened; the S&P 500 still gained about 2.5% over the week. Daily and weekly returns describe different windows.

Market impact (inference): Inference: large technology weights amplify index moves. Sector dispersion helps assess whether risk appetite is deteriorating broadly. Divergence suggests possible rotation but does not prove institutional inflows.

Countercase: Countercase: relative resilience offers no guarantee. Rising yields or earnings downgrades can also hit financials and defensive sectors. A weekly rebound does not establish a trend reversal.

Source provenance: Two independent news agencies and a separate closing report cross-check the moves. Multiple republications of the same wire count as one origin.

04 · Energy: improving supply outlook meets delivery risk

Facts: The November 13 International Energy Agency report forecasts a return to global oil surplus in Q4; this is a forecast, not a completed quarterly inventory result. The wire settlement report put Brent at $95.84 a barrel and US crude at $88.56, both down roughly 2.5% over the week.

Market impact (inference): Inference: recovering supply could reduce scarcity premiums and ease fuel users’ costs. Aggregate surplus does not guarantee the right products reach refiners, ports and consumers. Independent energy research also compares competing demand forecasts.

Countercase: Countercase: shipping, infrastructure and negotiation risks can quickly restore premiums. More oil at sea need not mean adequate onshore buffers. Intraday quotes are not settlements, and a forecast surplus does not establish an end to conflict.

Source provenance: The monthly report is the original forecast; the wire checks trading and events; the energy researcher compares independent forecasts. Its repetition of the monthly report is not an additional independent fact.

  • International wire energy closing report
  • Independent energy research and forecast comparison

05 · Company: European jewellery group grows, with adjustments

Facts: The European jewellery group with ticker CFR reported on November 13 that first-half sales through September were about €12.3 billion, up 17% at constant exchange rates. Operating profit was about €3.1 billion, or €2.9 billion excluding €229 million of positive tariff-related one-offs. Q2 jewellery sales grew 14% at constant rates.

Market impact (inference): Inference: sales and profit suggest resilient high-end demand, while valuation depends on incremental growth and sustainable earnings. An independent asset manager compared jewellery growth with a cited 16% consensus. That comparison is its analysis, not company guidance.

Countercase: Countercase: gold costs, currencies and regional demand differences can affect margins. Missing one expectation sample does not establish collapsing demand. One company cannot confirm a recovery across discretionary consumption.

Source provenance: The company release defines the figures; the Swiss news agency reports independently; the asset manager adds expectations and market interpretation. No unverified precise share-price decline is used.
