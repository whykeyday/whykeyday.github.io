01 / German wholesale prices: slower monthly gains, persistent annual pressure

Facts · Germany’s October wholesale prices, reported on November 13, rose 8.8% year on year after 8.7%. Monthly growth slowed to 0.4% from September’s 1.9%. These are wholesale prices, not consumer inflation.

Market impact · inference · Inference: upstream costs may squeeze industrial and retail margins and constrain expectations of European easing. The smaller monthly rise provides some relief.

Countercase and risks · Base effects influence annual rates, and pass-through to consumers varies. This release does not determine the ECB’s next move; slower monthly growth also does not mean falling prices.

Provenance and timing · Destatis is the statistical origin. Its calendar confirms timing only; Reuters, Anadolu and investingLive provide three separately edited checks of the figures.

05 / Oil: inventory rebuilding coexists with shipping risk

Facts · The EIA’s November 12 release, covering the week ended November 6, showed US commercial crude stocks up about 4.7 million barrels to 414.4 million, still 4% below the five-year average. Distillate stocks remained 8% below average. Reuters quoted Brent at $95.41 and WTI at $88.52 per barrel at 02:42 UTC on November 13, alongside weekly-loss prospects and shipping risks.

Market impact · inference · Inference: inventory rebuilding cushions supply concerns, but product buffers remain thin. Shipping and delivery costs can create divergent outcomes for airlines, chemicals and refiners.

Countercase and risks · Weekly stocks reflect imports, refinery operations and timing. Political statements do not establish physical flows, and early declines do not fix the final weekly return. The quoted prices are not live cutoff prices.

Provenance and timing · Sources are EIA’s original statistics, Reuters market reporting and Investing.com’s own commodities coverage. The latter’s 04:36 UTC quotes are a different observation and are not mixed with Reuters’ snapshot.

06 / Chip equipment earnings: revenue delivered, execution still matters

Facts · Applied Materials reported fiscal Q4 2026 revenue of $10.41 billion, up 53%, after the November 12 close. The quarter ended October 25. GAAP EPS was $3.40 and adjusted EPS was $4.16. Next-quarter revenue guidance is $11.10 billion, plus or minus $0.50 billion, and remains a company forecast.

Market impact · inference · Inference: equipment revenue offers tangible evidence of AI investment reaching manufacturing. Against volatile equities, actual deliveries, customer capital expenditure and stock valuation deserve separate assessment.

Countercase and risks · Customer concentration, export restrictions and capacity expansion can impede delivery. Non-GAAP earnings differ from GAAP. Strong historical growth guarantees neither guidance delivery nor a higher share price.

Provenance and timing · Company IR provides the original release; Reuters and GuruFocus offer separate market and call analysis. Company statements repeated in these reports remain one underlying origin, not three independent audit confirmations.
