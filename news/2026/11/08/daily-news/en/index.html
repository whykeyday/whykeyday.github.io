Reserve buffers and rising consumer credit put the recovery to an inflation test

Sunday 2026-11-08 | Verification cutoff 12:02 UTC / 07:02 EST, Toronto | Weekend edition

Sunday assessment: reserves and corporate buybacks provide cushions, while consumer borrowing, elevated rates and uncertain diesel deliveries constrain growth quality. Next week tests whether easing inflation, operating earnings and physical supply can support the equity recovery. This is an analytical judgment.

The latest verifiable public information covered here was mainly released November 6–8. U.S. cash equities are closed on Sunday; equity moves refer to the November 6 close, not November 8 trading.

Macro and reserves

01China reserves edge higher as gold volumes and value diverge

Released November 7 for end-October; additional reporting November 8

Facts

SAFE reported foreign exchange reserves of $3.4052 trillion, up $4.9 billion or 0.14% in October. Its table shows gold rising from 77.47 million to 78.30 million ounces while its dollar value fell from $323.517 billion to $306.411 billion.

Market implications | Inference

Stable reserves can cushion external financing pressure. Gold accumulation suggests continuing allocation demand, while the valuation decline separates physical purchases from price performance.

Countercase | Conditional risk

SAFE attributes the change to combined translation and asset-price effects. The increase does not establish net capital inflows or a stronger yuan ahead; reserve buying cannot guarantee higher gold prices.

Macro and household finance

02U.S. consumer credit accelerates as borrowing supports spending

Released November 6; preliminary September and third-quarter data

Facts

The Federal Reserve reports September consumer credit growth of 6.4% at a seasonally adjusted annual rate, including 14.5% for revolving credit. Total third-quarter growth was 4.2%. These are annualized rates, not monthly balance increases.

Market implications | Inference

Borrowing can sustain current spending and bank interest income. Greater reliance on revolving credit could make subsequent consumption more sensitive to financing costs.

Countercase | Conditional risk

Larger balances alone establish neither household distress nor rising defaults. Income and delinquency data matter; steady employment could support repayment.

Sources and definitions

  • Federal Reserve G.19 release — 2026-11-06
  • KPMG consumer credit analysis — 2026-11-06
  • ABA Banking Journal credit report — 2026-11-06

Three organizations: Federal Reserve statistics, KPMG analysis and the banking association. The association prints 6.2% for September, conflicting with the official 6.4%, which is used here. The series excludes real-estate-secured loans.

Equities rates and currencies

03Equity recovery remains uneven as yields and the dollar constrain risk

November 6 U.S. close and contemporaneous FX; October 28 policy background

Facts

On Friday the S&P 500 rose 0.31%, Nasdaq gained about 1.1% and Dow fell 0.46%; all lost ground for the week. Treasury recorded a 4.86% ten-year par yield versus 4.84% Thursday. Reuters reported the euro at $1.1142. The October 28 Fed decision maintained a 3.75%–4.00% target range.

Market implications | Inference

The technology recovery coexists with higher discount rates, keeping earnings delivery relevant. A stronger dollar could pressure non-U.S. companies funded in dollars.

Countercase | Conditional risk

One rebound cannot establish a global recovery in risk appetite, and one yield increase cannot establish another policy hike. Softer inflation and stronger earnings could ease financing constraints.

Energy and transport

04Russian diesel diversions leave supply relief dependent on delivery

November 7 reporting; November 2 port notice as background

Facts

CNN and Le Monde report diversions of two Russian diesel tankers previously heading for the U.S.; another cargo has no assured discharge. New Jersey warned port operators on November 2 that imports conflict with federal law. This is the state’s legal position, not a judicial finding by this publication.

Market implications | Inference

Without willing buyers, insurers and receiving ports, authorized supply may not quickly lower end-user costs, leaving fuel budgets under pressure.

Countercase | Conditional risk

Diversion does not remove barrels from global supply. Other buyers and trade rerouting could ease tightness; vessel plans and policy disputes may change.

Sources and definitions

  • CNN diesel shipment reporting via KQ2 — 2026-11-07
  • Le Monde diesel delivery report — 2026-11-07
  • New Jersey official port operator notice — 2026-11-02 background

CNN counts once; KQ2 distributes its reporting. Le Monde partly cites other media for shipping information, not another independent tracking check. The state notice independently establishes a dispute, not completed delivery.

Company and insurance

05Berkshire expands buybacks while underwriting tests operating quality

Released November 7; third-quarter 2026 results

Facts

Berkshire reports operating earnings of $12.705 billion versus $13.485 billion a year earlier and approximately $9.6 billion in quarterly buybacks. Insurance underwriting earnings fell from $2.369 billion to $1.352 billion; railroad and manufacturing, service and retailing results improved.

Market implications | Inference

Buybacks reduce shares outstanding, while underwriting and operating businesses determine underlying earnings quality. Cash returns and profit growth remain separate tests.

Countercase | Conditional risk

Claims and pricing cycles can make underwriting volatile, and other businesses may cushion weakness. Conversely, repurchases cannot establish cheap valuation or offset persistent operating deterioration.

Sources and definitions

Three source chains: company IR, Reuters and Associated Press; distributors are not extra origins. Operating earnings are company-defined non-GAAP figures. Net income includes investment valuation movements and is not a direct operating trend.
