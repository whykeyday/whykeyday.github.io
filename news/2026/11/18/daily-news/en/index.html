UK inflation picks up as Asian chip shares rebound

2026-11-18 · As of 07:02 ET (UTC−5)

UK goods inflation strengthened, while Japanese chip-related exports and a U.S. chip supplier’s sales point to resilient demand for computing infrastructure. Asian equity markets diverged, and the holiday retail test is just beginning.

UK inflation rises to 3.6%, with core and services steady

Facts · On November 18, the Office for National Statistics reported that October CPI rose 3.6% from a year earlier, up from 3.5% and the highest in a year. Goods inflation rose from 3.2% to 3.5%, while core and services inflation held at 2.8% and 3.7%, respectively. On November 5, the Bank of England announced a 25-basis-point increase to 4%.

Market implications (inference) · If higher goods prices feed into wages and services, UK short-term rate expectations could rise and the yield differential could support sterling.

Countervailing risks · The acceleration was concentrated in goods. If energy prices fall and services inflation stays steady, the headline rise offers limited evidence for another hike.

Sources · Office for National Statistics CPI release · Bank of England policy report · Independent reporting on UK inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield eases to 5.10% ahead of Fed minutes

Facts · The U.S. Treasury’s 10-year benchmark yield was 5.10% on November 17, down two basis points from November 16. Early market reporting on November 18 put the dollar at about ¥159.22, down from about ¥159.40, an intraday quote. The Fed is due to release its October 27–28 minutes today at 2:00 p.m. ET. The committee voted 10–2 to hold its 3.75%–4.00% target range, with both dissenters favoring a 25-basis-point hike.

Market implications (inference) · Evidence of wider support for tightening could lift front-end yields and the dollar and weigh on the valuations of long-duration equities.

Countervailing risks · The minutes describe last month’s discussion. Newer data could shift the policy outlook, and demand for safety could pull long yields down even if front-end yields rise.

Sources · Federal Reserve calendar · Federal Reserve’s October 28 statement · U.S. Treasury yield curve · Intraday FX and Treasury reporting

Japan’s exports hit a record as imports grow faster

Facts · Japan’s Ministry of Finance reported on November 18 that provisional October exports rose 20.5% year over year to ¥11.77 trillion, their 14th consecutive monthly increase on a year-over-year basis. Imports rose 21.6% to ¥12.25 trillion, leaving an unadjusted trade deficit of ¥476.5 billion. Semiconductor-related demand supported exports, while energy costs lifted the import bill.

Market implications (inference) · The export figures offer a demand signal for Japanese semiconductor equipment suppliers. Higher imported energy costs could squeeze margins at energy-intensive firms.

Countervailing risks · These are nominal yen values. Currency moves and prices can lift the totals, so shipment volumes and orders will help assess whether demand can hold up.

Sources · Japan Ministry of Finance provisional trade figures · Independent reporting on energy imports · Wire reporting on semiconductor-related exports

Korean shares rebound while Japanese benchmarks diverge

Facts · At the November 18 Asian close, the KOSPI rose 1.99% to 5,067.44. The Nikkei 225 gained 0.28%, while the broader TOPIX fell 0.42%. At the November 17 U.S. close, the S&P 500 fell about 1.0% to 7,340.53 and the Nasdaq Composite lost about 0.7%.

Market implications (inference) · The chip rebound and the split between Japan’s two benchmarks suggest buying favored selected technology shares. The next checks are whether more stocks join the advance and whether it carries into the U.S. session.

Countervailing risks · These closes reflect different information windows. The prior U.S. close preceded NVDA’s results. The rebound could fade if yields rise or earnings upgrades stay confined to a handful of stocks.

Sources · U.S. benchmark closing report · Korean close and intraday trading · Asian benchmark closing report · Tokyo close and market breadth

NVDA revenue doubles as next-quarter margin guidance steps down

Facts · After the U.S. close on November 17, NVDA reported fiscal third-quarter 2027 revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year. Data center revenue rose 110% to $107.6 billion. Management guided to $130 billion in fourth-quarter revenue, plus or minus 3%, and a non-GAAP gross margin of 71%–72%, compared with 74.9% in the third quarter.

Market implications (inference) · Data center sales show that infrastructure spending translated into deliveries in the quarter and give memory and equipment suppliers a signal about demand.

Countervailing risks · The margin outlook points to a possible gap between revenue growth and profitability. Customer financing, the durability of spending and product mix will matter for earnings across the supply chain.

Sources · NVDA investor relations release · Independent reporting on earnings and outlook · Bank research on guidance and estimates

TGT comparable sales rise 5.6% as tariff refunds lift EPS

Facts · Before the U.S. open on November 18, TGT reported that third-quarter net sales rose 7.1% year over year to about $27.1 billion. Comparable sales rose 5.6% and comparable traffic rose 5.0%. GAAP and adjusted earnings per share were both $2.65, each including a $0.50 benefit from tariff refunds. The company raised its full-year net sales growth outlook by one percentage point to around 6%.

Market implications (inference) · Growth in both traffic and comparable sales gives this retailer momentum heading into the holiday shopping season.

Countervailing risks · Adjusted EPS still includes the refund. Holiday discounting could erode margins, and this retailer’s traffic gains could reflect shifting market share.

Sources · TGT investor relations release · Independent reporting on sales and refunds · Independent reporting on traffic and the holiday season
