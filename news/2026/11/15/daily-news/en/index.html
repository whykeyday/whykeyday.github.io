Macro / fiscal policy

01 Germany advances an expansionary budget, with a funding trade-off

Facts

On November 13, the German parliament’s budget committee approved a 2027 plan with €560.92bn in core spending, €61.85bn in investment and €122.97bn in net core borrowing. Reuters puts borrowing including special funds at about €210.4bn. These are different measures, and committee approval is still short of final parliamentary passage.

Market implications · inference

Inference: infrastructure and defence spending could support orders and European demand. Greater bond supply could also raise long-term financing costs. Equity benefits depend on procurement, execution and profits, rather than the headline spending envelope alone.

Counterpoint / risk

Counterpoint / risk: fiscal support may cushion weak external demand, but permitting bottlenecks, construction capacity and rising costs can weaken the multiplier. Borrowing does not substitute for structural reform.

Rates / currencies

02 Higher Treasury yields keep the dollar in focus

Facts

AP’s Friday closing report put the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield at 5.15%, up from 5.10% late Thursday. A November 13 Reuters intraday report described the dollar near a 19-month high and sterling heading for its largest weekly fall since September; these are intraday observations, not final weekly fixes. Background: the Fed held its target range at 3.75%–4.00% on October 28.

Market implications · inference

Inference: higher risk-free rates raise valuation hurdles and refinancing costs; a firmer dollar can burden some borrowers outside the United States. Yield moves reflect policy expectations, inflation and term premium, and cannot be read solely as the next central-bank decision.

Counterpoint / risk

Counterpoint / risk: weaker growth or spending could reverse yields and the dollar. Other central banks and safe-haven demand also affect currencies. Market expectations of tightening are not an announced rate increase.

Equities / fund flows

03 A U.S. weekly rebound masks tech weakness as Europe attracts funds

Facts

At the November 13 close, the S&P 500 fell about 0.4%, the Nasdaq Composite 0.9%, while the Dow gained about 0.1%; the main U.S. indexes still rose for the week. In a separate window, European equity funds took in $35.13bn and global equity funds $50.99bn in the week to November 11, Reuters reported using LSEG Lipper data. Fund subscriptions are neither exchange net buying nor Friday-only flows.

Market implications · inference

Inference: risk appetite is diverging across regions and sectors. A weekly rebound does not establish that technology stress has passed. Equal-weight performance, sector breadth and earnings delivery offer useful checks on the quality of the recovery.

Counterpoint / risk

Counterpoint / risk: a single week can reflect institutional rebalancing or large subscriptions. Europe remains exposed to energy costs and global growth. Rotation can reverse and does not establish persistent European outperformance.

Industry / energy regulation

04 North Sea approvals ruled invalid, without an automatic production halt

Facts

On November 13, Norway’s Supreme Court found procedural defects in approvals concerning Breidablikk, Tyrving and Yggdrasil. Assessments must cover emissions from burning the sold oil and gas; a 6–5 majority required consideration alongside other approved projects’ cumulative emissions. Operators were not parties, so their permissions do not automatically lapse. The ministry must reassess the approvals.

Market implications · inference

Inference: assessment costs, approval timelines and litigation risk may rise, affecting capital-spending schedules. The judgment does not quantify lost supply and does not itself establish an oil-price rise or supply disruption.

Counterpoint / risk

Counterpoint / risk: projects may proceed after further assessment. Reuters reported that the ministry expected production and development to remain unaffected, which is an attributed expectation. Reapproval and possible further litigation remain uncertain.

Company / networking equipment

05 Networking growth meets a margin and valuation test

Facts

Cisco disclosed fiscal Q1 2027 revenue of $18.5bn, up 24%, for the quarter ended October 24, and raised full-year revenue guidance to $74.8bn–$75.8bn on November 12. Non-GAAP gross margin fell to 65.6% from 68.1%, while non-GAAP operating margin was 37.8%. Kiplinger reported a 6.2% Friday share decline. Guidance is a forecast; non-GAAP measures do not replace GAAP results.

Market implications · inference

Inference: data-centre investment can support networking demand, but sales growth, gross margin and operating margin need separate analysis. Expense efficiency can support operating margins. A one-day share fall does not demonstrate weaker customer demand.

Counterpoint / risk

Counterpoint / risk: sustained orders and cost control may offset component pressure. Higher financing costs or delayed customer construction could undermine guidance. An earnings beat does not guarantee a higher valuation.
