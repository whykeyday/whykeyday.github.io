Daily Finance | November 11, 2026 | Technology rebounds under inflation and funding pressure

As of November 11, 2026, 07:00 EST (Toronto), or 12:00 UTC. By Daily Finance.

Sticky core inflation raises the rate hurdle while AI infrastructure earnings support Asian equities. The useful distinction is between delivered demand, funding costs and cash conversion, rather than a single direction for all assets.

“Facts” include published data and explicitly attributed forecasts. “Market impact” is analytical inference; “Counter-risk” describes conditional scenarios. November 10 releases inform today’s market assessment. All intraday snapshots are distinct from closing prices.

1. US core inflation stays firm as some annual comparisons disappear

Facts and timing — Released on November 10 at 08:30 ET, October CPI rose 0.4% month over month, seasonally adjusted; core CPI rose 0.3% for a third month. Missing October 2025 observations prevent publication of some annual changes, including headline and core CPI. Reuters consensus was 0.3% and 0.2%, respectively.

Market impact — Inference: Slower energy inflation has yet to cool core prices, raising the earnings hurdle for rate-sensitive assets.

Counter-risk — Counter-risk: One month and a missing comparison base limit trend judgments. Softer services prices or demand could reverse rate expectations.

Sources — BLS · CPI · 2026-11-10 08:30 ET · Reuters · 2026-11-10 US session · Axios · 2026-11-10

2. Bonds and the dollar absorb larger-hike risk ahead of a US bond holiday

Facts and timing — Before the November 10 release, a Fed governor said a core CPI reading around 0.3% or higher would warrant considering a 50-basis-point December hike. This was an individual view. Reuters subsequently reported rising Treasury yields and a stronger dollar. November 11 is a recommended US bond-market holiday; prior-session yields are not live holiday trades.

Market impact — Inference: Dollar funding and discount-rate pressure may rise together; the bond holiday weakens cross-asset confirmation.

Counter-risk — Counter-risk: Neither one official nor market pricing commits the committee. Other policymakers, jobs and inflation can change the path.

Sources — Federal Reserve · speech · 2026-11-10 · Reuters · 2026-11-10 US session · SIFMA · 2026 holiday schedule

3. Asian technology stocks rebound while rate pressure leaves markets divided

Facts and timing — In its November 11 update at 01:26 ET, Reuters reported gains of about 1.9% for a broad Asia-Pacific index, 2.6% in South Korea, 3% in Taiwan and 1.5% in Japan. These are intraday snapshots. Bloomberg opening coverage and local Korean reporting also described improved sentiment after earnings; their differently timed figures are not interchangeable.

Market impact — Inference: Computing demand can support direct suppliers even as bond pressure persists; equity gains do not establish broadly easier financing.

Counter-risk — Counter-risk: A narrow technology-led rebound may fade if funding costs keep rising.

Sources — Reuters · 2026-11-11 01:26 ET update · Bloomberg · 2026-11-11 Asia opening report · ChosunBiz · 2026-11-11

4. Energy forecasts lag the latest risk repricing as diesel remains a separate issue

Facts and timing — The official November energy outlook, released November 10, forecasts US retail diesel averaging $5.03 per gallon in 2027, about 12% above the previous forecast. Model inputs closed November 5. Separate November 10 reporting described a falling crude risk premium. These differ in timing and scope; neither is a live retail quote today.

Market impact — Inference: Transport and industrial companies cannot assume falling crude immediately lowers fuel bills; refining and shipping constraints affect margins.

Counter-risk — Counter-risk: Better shipping and product supply could lower forecasts; renewed conflict would create the opposite shock.

Sources — EIA · November outlook · released 2026-11-10 · Axios · energy report · 2026-11-10 · Kiplinger · energy outlook · 2026-11-09

5. CoreWeave raises revenue guidance while financing remains the earnings hurdle

Facts and timing — November 10 after-close results show Q3 revenue of $3.661 billion, a $709 million net loss and $846 million in net interest expense. Full-year revenue guidance rose to $13.0–13.3 billion, with capital spending expected at $37–39 billion. Adjusted profitability in the first half of 2028 is a company target, not a result.

Market impact — Inference: Delivered revenue eases near-term demand concerns, but financing sustainability requires attention to interest and cash investment alongside adjusted operations.

Counter-risk — Counter-risk: Prepayments and delivery could ease funding pressure. Delays, customer concentration or costly refinancing could undermine growth.

Sources — CoreWeave · Q3 release · 2026-11-10 after close · Reuters · 2026-11-10 20:19 ET update · Investing.com · 2026-11-11 Australia edition

6. Vestas improves margins while orders and cash conversion weaken

Facts and timing — The November 11 release at 07:23 CET shows Q3 revenue of €5.677 billion and an EBIT margin before special items of 8.5%, versus 7.8% a year earlier. Turbine orders fell 27% to 3,377 MW; adjusted free cash flow fell to €99 million from €508 million. The annual margin range narrowed from 7–9% to 7.5–8.5%, leaving its midpoint unchanged.

Market impact — Inference: Better delivery profitability does not establish stronger future growth when orders and cash conversion weaken. A narrower range is not automatically an upgrade.

Counter-risk — Counter-risk: Contract timing can distort quarterly orders. Pricing and service revenue offer support, while offshore execution costs remain uncertain.

Sources — Vestas · Q3 report · 2026-11-11 07:23 CET · Reuters · 2026-11-11 07:56 CET · Finwire · 2026-11-11 12:05 IST
