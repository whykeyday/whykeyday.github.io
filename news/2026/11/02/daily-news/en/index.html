01 / 06

China manufacturing: expansion with softer exports and pricing

Facts — The private manufacturing PMI released November 2 eased to 52.0 in October from 52.1 in September, remaining above 50. Reuters reports slower export-order growth, the fastest output growth in six months and renewed declines in selling prices. The official PMI released over the weekend was 49.3. Different samples and weights mean the two indices cannot be averaged into national output growth.

Market impact | Inference — Inference: Expanding production can support industrial revenue, but volume growth need not improve profits. Export orders and selling prices are useful tests of demand quality.

Counter-risk — Counter-risk: Sector mix, business size and holidays may explain divergence. Faster cost declines could allow profits to improve despite price cuts. Neither a broad recession nor a broad recovery is established.

02 / 06

China changes its operating anchor: gross liquidity is not net easing

Facts — On November 2, the central bank conducted CNY541 billion of overnight reverse repos at 1.25%. Its October 30 notice introduced daily fixed-rate overnight operations from today and changed seven-day operations to multiple-rate bidding. CNY541 billion is a gross operation amount; this edition has not reconciled maturities and does not describe it as a net liquidity injection.

Market impact | Inference — Inference: A shorter operating anchor may improve funding-price transmission. Institutions interviewed by Reuters disagree about the degree of effective easing, so the change does not establish a lending-rate cut.

Counter-risk — Counter-risk: Credit concerns and weak borrowing demand may limit transmission. Actual funding-cost declines could also make the effect stronger than a technical adjustment suggests. Watch overnight rates and loan pricing.

04 / 06

Equities stabilize: futures rebound still needs a cash-market test

Facts — The Reuters November 2 snapshot at 09:15–09:16 UTC showed Nasdaq futures up 0.76%, S&P 500 futures up 0.34% and the STOXX 600 about 0.05% higher, with Korea stronger and Japan weaker. AP also reported mostly firmer Asian shares but divergent technology stocks. US cash trading had not opened; this is not a Monday closing rebound.

Market impact | Inference — Inference: Relief in energy costs can support sentiment, while cash returns on technology spending still matter for breadth. Advancing shares and sector participation after the cash open are more informative than futures alone.

Counter-risk — Counter-risk: Earnings disappointments or new shocks may reverse the rebound; stronger profits and broader buying could sustain it. Quotes from different times are not one synchronized snapshot.

05 / 06

Energy: unchanged December targets as diplomacy weighs on oil

Facts — The November 1 official statement confirms that seven producers kept December required production at September levels and will meet again November 29. AP reported Brent and US benchmark crude down about 3.3% in early Asian trading on November 2 amid diplomatic hopes. Reopening the shipping route remained a conditional proposal, not an accomplished fact.

Market impact | Inference — Inference: The decline is consistent with repricing the risk premium rather than a newly announced production increase. Airlines, transport and chemicals depend on actual deliveries and purchase costs.

Counter-risk — Counter-risk: Failed talks or renewed shipping disruptions could lift prices; restored transit and weak demand could extend declines. Stable quotas do not guarantee stable exports.

06 / 06

Bank earnings: credit costs offset lending growth

Facts — Westpac released results on November 2 for the year ended September 30: statutory net profit was about A$6.8 billion, down 1%; profit excluding Notable Items was about A$7.0 billion, broadly flat. The final dividend was A$0.77 per share. Loans rose 5%, deposits 6%, and credit impairment charges increased to A$864 million. All amounts are Australian dollars.

Market impact | Inference — Inference: Lending growth and capital provide support, but credit costs show that high rates are not an unqualified benefit for banks. Pre-provision earnings, funding competition and asset quality all matter.

Counter-risk — Counter-risk: Impairments include forward-looking scenarios and overlays, rather than only realized cash losses. Better conditions could ease pressure, while household stress could require more provisions. Separate news reports citing one filing are not separate audits.
