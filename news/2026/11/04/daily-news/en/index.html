After election repricing, growth and delivery take over

Equity futures recover early losses as Treasury yields and the dollar weaken. Slower Chinese and European services contrast with strong data-center growth. Separate policy expectations, actual demand and corporate delivery.

November 4, 2026 · Verification cutoff 12:02 UTC / 07:02 EST (Toronto)

01 / Equities / Rates / FX

Stocks recover early losses as Treasury yields and the dollar fall

Facts

Media reported Democratic majorities in both congressional chambers on November 4; this brief has not verified final certification. Reuters’ 04:09 EST snapshot showed US stock futures turning positive after overnight losses of up to 2%, the STOXX 600 up about 0.5%, and the 10-year Treasury yield down about 3 basis points to 4.92%. These are intraday observations, not a US cash close.

Market impact | Inference

Inference: expectations of fiscal restraint and technology regulation can lower yields while driving equity rotation. Dollar weakness need not lift every risk asset, and a futures recovery does not resolve capital-spending risks.

Counter-risk | Scenario

Counter-risk: legislative procedures and executive powers constrain policy change. Jobs, inflation and earnings may reverse initial pricing. Lower bond yields are not a central-bank rate cut.

02 / Macro / China

China’s services expansion slows as pricing power weakens

Facts

October’s private services PMI, released November 4, fell to 50.8 from 51.6; the composite eased to 52.0 from 52.4. Above 50 still indicates monthly expansion. Three data or news desks describe slower growth and rising costs alongside price discounting. The survey is not a national GDP growth rate.

Market impact | Inference

Inference: volume expansion need not produce profit growth. Services, transport and export businesses require checks on orders, cost pass-through and cash collection. Manufacturing alone cannot establish the broader growth picture.

Counter-risk | Scenario

Counter-risk: new business is still growing; policy support or external demand could stabilize activity. Sample composition and monthly noise matter. The original survey release was inaccessible; verification relies on cross-reporting.

03 / Macro / Europe / Central banks

Euro-area PMI revision signals slower growth and policy trade-offs

Facts

The euro-area October composite PMI finalized on November 4 at 51.7, below the 51.9 flash estimate and September’s 53.1. Services registered 51.4 versus 53.0 in September. Reuters, a data provider and an independent economic-news calendar agree on these readings. The final indices remain above 50: expansion slowed rather than turning into contraction.

Market impact | Inference

Inference: weaker growth complicates further monetary tightening, but a survey cannot determine the next ECB decision. European equities require separate assessments of demand and cost pass-through; modest expansion does not imply broad profit improvement.

Counter-risk | Scenario

Counter-risk: surveys may understate output resilience. Persistent price pressure could prevent easing despite slower growth. Flash estimates, final surveys and official output data contain different information.

04 / Sector / Energy

Oil reverses early gains as diplomacy meets physical supply risk

Facts

Reuters quoted Brent futures at $104.42 at 05:10 UTC on November 4 and $103.17 in its 04:09 EST update. A separate data page showed a WTI-tracking CFD near $95.30 when checked: a different instrument and timestamp. The EIA’s October 6 outlook assumed constrained fourth-quarter Middle East flows; that is forecast context, not new supply data today.

Market impact | Inference

Inference: diplomacy can rapidly reduce risk premia; transport and refinery recovery determine whether cost relief lasts. Cheaper crude may help consumers and logistics without proving that diesel shortages or inflation are over.

Counter-risk | Scenario

Counter-risk: outages, shipping disruption or failed negotiations could lift prices; weaker demand could deepen losses. Official inventories were still pending and cannot be replaced with industry estimates.

05 / Company / Semiconductors

Chip results show data-center strength, with guidance still to deliver

Facts

AMD’s November 3 after-close release reported roughly $13.4 billion in third-quarter revenue, up 45%; data-center revenue rose 97% to about $8.5 billion, while gaming revenue fell 40%. Fourth-quarter revenue guidance of about $17 billion, plus or minus 3%, is forward-looking. Reuters reported a roughly 7.3% premarket gain at 04:52 EST on November 4, not a closing return.

Market impact | Inference

Inference: data-center growth supports computing demand, while segment divergence cautions against extrapolating AI spending to all chip markets. Deliveries, customer spending and earnings quality remain tests.

Counter-risk | Scenario

Counter-risk: supply constraints, export rules, customer concentration and valuation expectations may offset growth. Delivery execution or market-share gains could support earnings. Premarket gains can reverse.
