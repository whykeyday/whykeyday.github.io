Robe (Kosode) with Pines and Interlocking Squares

A Line of Warmth at the Hem

Pine boughs and diamonds, made luminous by tiny ties in the silk.

Unidentified Japanese maker
Japan, Edo period · Second half of the 18th century · Tie-dyed figured silk satin · 180.3 × 127 cm

Composition · A steady outline, a pattern on the move

Start with the silhouette. The sleeves open into a long horizontal, and the skirt widens toward the hem. Against that almost symmetrical outline, squares turn onto their corners and run diagonally across the sleeve, back and hem. Pine boughs spread over the shoulders and return lower down. The two motifs meet at different heights, so the eye keeps changing direction as it crosses the cloth.

Color · Cool field, warm edge

Deep blue and muted blue-gray share a cool hue family. The value jump from deep blue to pale ivory lets tiny marks gather into motifs you can read at a distance. The orange-red trim sits near blue’s complement. Its narrow run along the hem and sleeve edges gives that small dose of warmth an unusually clear place to land.

Light · Brightness in the dye and in the photograph

The pale marks within the pines and squares are areas spared by the dye. In the collection photograph, soft changes in brightness also describe the folds, while the shadow below the hem separates the robe from the gray backdrop. The dyed marks tell you where the pattern goes. The photographed light tells you how the cloth hangs.

Technique and maker · A resist made by hand

The collection dates the robe to the second half of the eighteenth century in Edo-period Japan. Its maker is unidentified. In kanoko shibori, small pinches of silk are bound before dyeing. Once the ties come off, the resisted spots emerge, flecked like a fawn’s coat. The collection notes that garments covered in this labor-intensive work formed part of bridal trousseaux in wealthy Kyoto merchant families.

Why it moves me · A trace of the hand

From a distance the grid looks exact. Up close, the light spots vary slightly in shape. I keep coming back to that small variation. Even after repeating the same gesture across the cloth, a pair of hands could still leave a different mark with each tie.

Try it on · Save the orange for last

Pair a deep blue jacket with blue-gray trousers and ivory knitwear. Let a sliver of orange-red show at a sock cuff or scarf edge. Start with a visible-area split of 60 / 25 / 12 / 3. For a nod to the pattern, keep a diagonal check to one piece and give it room with solids. The hex codes are suggested styling colors.
