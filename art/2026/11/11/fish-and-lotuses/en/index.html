A pond in the palm

Bowl with fish and lotuses
Unidentified Egyptian maker · c. 1550–1300 BCE · Faience

Follow the fish from tail to mouth, then let the two stems carry your eye outward. A small bowl turns the idea of being alive into a route you can travel again.

Composition | A circle that breathes

The rim encloses the image; the upright fish establishes its axis. Two stems spread above the mouth, while the hanging buds draw attention down again. The arrangement approaches symmetry without making each side identical. Small differences keep the order supple. Blue gaps between body, stems and rim prevent the design from becoming a solid badge. This describes its visual effect, rather than a documented intention.

Colour | Blue becomes space

In the photograph, cyan shifts towards greenish blue: a narrow range of neighbouring cool hues. The near-black marks create value contrast rather than a complementary colour scheme. A darker rim contains a lighter interior, helping the eye and spots register. Scattered brown traces interrupt the blue, but a photograph alone cannot establish which surface changes belong to the original decoration.

Light | A shallow depth

The fish has little tonal modelling. Contour and dots carry its shape. Depth comes largely from the vessel: the dark rim, changes of brightness across the interior and the granular glaze make the drawn fish seem suspended in shallow water. This is an effect of material and photographic lighting; it does not establish how the object was lit in antiquity.

Technique and maker | The hand remains

The maker is unidentified. The object belongs to an ancient Egyptian craft tradition; no individual biography can responsibly be supplied. Faience combines a silica-rich body with a glaze. Technical literature explains that copper can produce its characteristic turquoise colour. Here, alternating fine lines and solid marks keep the small design legible. The precise forming and glazing sequence for this bowl is unconfirmed.

Why it moves | Room for another beginning

The collection interprets fish and lotus as signs of regeneration. My response begins with the fish itself, held between two soft arcs. A wish of enormous scope has been brought close to the hand. Its tenderness comes through economical marks: the image needs very little to suggest that life might continue.

Try it | Let the ground flow

Build a vertical poster around a low circle, one upright shape and two outward curves. Leave at least a third of the circle empty. Try 70% cyan #38A6BF, 20% mist #E7ECEA and 10% ink #263C42. Let cyan hold space, mist create pauses and ink define only the main contour, title and a few dots. These are editorial exercise colours and proportions, not measurements or an ancient recipe.
